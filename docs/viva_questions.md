## Section 1: Team & Architecture Overview

### Q1: What is DemandIQ 2.0, and what core business problem does it solve?
**Answer:** DemandIQ 2.0 is an enterprise-grade retail demand forecasting and inventory decision-support platform. It solves the retail dilemma of balancing stockout risks (lost revenue, damaged goodwill) against overstocking costs (capital lockup, holding charges, severe markdowns). It integrates chronologically validated XGBoost forecasting, transparent heuristic baselines, deterministic inventory replenishment policies, and hallucination-proof AI insights.

### Q2: How did the team of 4 divide responsibilities to build this platform?
**Answer:** The team divided the architecture into four decoupled, testable domains:
1. *Member 1 (Data & Analytics):* Database models, synthetic dataset generation, time-series aggregation, and seasonality analytics.
2. *Member 2 (ML & Forecasting):* Feature engineering without leakage, chronological train/test split, XGBoost modeling, baseline benchmarking, and WAPE evaluation.
3. *Member 3 (Backend & Decisions):* FastAPI REST architecture, Pydantic v2 schemas, inventory replenishment formulas, and pricing rules.
4. *Member 4 (Frontend & AI Assistant):* React 19 UI, Recharts dashboards, structured AI insights engine, and guardrailed Business Assistant.

### Q3: What is the provenance of the underlying dataset?
**Answer:** The dataset is 100% synthetically generated (`is_synthetic = True`, `data_source = "DemandIQ_Synthetic_Retail_v2"`) using a fixed random seed (`seed=42`) to ensure full scientific reproducibility. It spans **48 consecutive calendar months from January 1, 2021 through December 31, 2024**, covering 8,048 multi-category raw transactions across 20 stable SKUs in 4 categories (*Beauty*, *Clothing*, *Electronics*, *Home*).

### Q4: Why is January 2025 explicitly labeled as a "Historical Prototype Forecast"?
**Answer:** Because the historical transactional records terminate on December 31, 2024. All next-period predictions generated by the platform represent an out-of-sample January 2025 forecast evaluated against December 2024 actuals. Transparently labeling this as a *Historical Prototype Forecast* prevents evaluators or users from mistaking it for live, real-world sales predictions for current calendar dates.

## Section 2: Data Engineering & Analytics (Member 1 Lead)

### Q5: How is data persisted in DemandIQ 2.0, and why was SQLite chosen?
**Answer:** Data is persisted in an embedded SQLite database using SQLAlchemy 2.0 ORM. The central table `sales_records` indexes `order_date`, `sku_id`, `product_category`, and temporal features. SQLite was chosen because it provides a self-contained, zero-configuration, ACID-compliant relational engine that guarantees reproducible test suites without external database container dependencies.

### Q6: How do you verify mathematical consistency in the sales transactions?
**Answer:** An automated test in `test_dataset_validation.py` asserts the fundamental revenue identity:
$$\text{Revenue} = \text{Quantity} \times \text{Unit Price} \times (1 - \text{Discount})$$
It verifies that for all 8,048 records, the absolute difference between recorded revenue and calculated revenue is less than $\$0.01$, ensuring zero rounding errors or corrupt financial records.

### Q7: How are raw transactions aggregated into monthly category panels for ML?
**Answer:** In `backend/app/analytics.py` and `ml.py`, raw transaction timestamps are truncated to `year_month` periods (`dt.to_period("M")`). Pandas performs a vectorized `groupby(["product_category", "year_month"])` aggregating `quantity` with `.sum()`, `unit_price` with `.mean()`, and promotional/holiday flags with `.sum()`.

### Q8: How does the system calculate Month-over-Month (MoM) product momentum?
**Answer:** In `get_trending_products()`, demand is aggregated for the latest month ($M_t = \text{2024-12}$) and prior month ($M_{t-1} = \text{2024-11}$):
$$\text{Growth}_{\text{MoM}} = \frac{Q_t - Q_{t-1}}{Q_{t-1}} \times 100\%$$
Products with growth $> +5\%$ are classified as *Accelerating* (e.g., Jacket in Clothing), $< -5\%$ as *Decelerating*, and between $-5\%$ and $+5\%$ as *Stable*.

### Q9: How is the Seasonality Index calculated and interpreted?
**Answer:** In `get_seasonality()`, the monthly seasonality index is defined as:
$$\text{Index}_m = \left(\frac{\bar{D}_m}{\bar{D}_{\text{annual}}}\right) \times 100$$
Where $\bar{D}_m$ is the average monthly sales for calendar month $m$ across all 4 years, and $\bar{D}_{\text{annual}}$ is the overall average monthly sales. An index of $180$ indicates demand is $80\%$ above normal baseline (e.g., Sunscreen in July), while an index of $60$ indicates demand is $40\%$ below normal.

### Q10: How does the analytics engine handle event and holiday demand lift?
**Answer:** It compares average daily unit sales on days where `holiday_flag == True` against days where `holiday_flag == False`:
$$\text{Lift}_{\text{holiday}} = \frac{\bar{D}_{\text{holiday}} - \bar{D}_{\text{normal}}}{\bar{D}_{\text{normal}}} \times 100\%$$
In the historical dataset, Electronics exhibits an observed holiday lift of $+18.4\%$, reflecting Q4 promotional shopping.

### Q11: What is the Multi-Level Granularity Reconciliation in DemandIQ?
**Answer:** The dataset spans three reconciled operational layers:
1. *Raw Transaction Layer:* 8,048 customer purchases across 2021–2024.
2. *Monthly SKU Layer:* 960 records ($48 \text{ months} \times 20 \text{ SKUs}$), partitioned into 60 warm-up ($3 \times 20$), 720 training ($36 \times 20$), and 180 test records ($9 \times 20$).
3. *Category Forecasting Layer:* 192 records ($48 \text{ months} \times 4 \text{ categories}$), partitioned into 12 warm-up ($3 \times 4$), 144 training ($36 \times 4$), and 36 test records ($9 \times 4$).

## Section 3: Machine Learning & Forecasting (Member 2 Lead)

### Q12: Why is random k-fold cross-validation strictly forbidden in time-series forecasting?
**Answer:** Random k-fold cross-validation shuffles data across time, allowing future observations ($t+1, t+5$) to appear in training splits while predicting past observations ($t$). This creates severe *look-ahead data leakage*, artificially deflating error metrics and leading to catastrophic model failures in production. DemandIQ enforces strict chronological splitting.

### Q13: How is the chronological train/test split structured in DemandIQ 2.0?
**Answer:**
- *Feature Warm-Up:* 2021-01 to 2021-03 (3 months dropped solely to initialize 3-month lag and rolling features).
- *Chronological Training Set (80%):* 2021-04 to 2024-03 (36 consecutive months, $N=36$ per category).
- *Chronological Hold-Out Test Set (20%):* 2024-04 to 2024-12 (9 consecutive months, $N=9$ per category).
The test split is completely untouched during model training and hyperparameter tuning.

### Q14: How does DemandIQ mathematically prevent data leakage during feature engineering?
**Answer:** Features are derived strictly from preceding periods:
- `lag1`: `total_quantity.shift(1)` $\implies y_{t-1}$.
- `lag2`: `total_quantity.shift(2)` $\implies y_{t-2}$.
- `rolling3`: `total_quantity.shift(1).rolling(3).mean()` $\implies \frac{y_{t-1} + y_{t-2} + y_{t-3}}{3}$.
The shift barrier ensures the target value $y_t$ is never included in the rolling window. Unit test `test_feature_leakage_prevention` proves that altering $y_t$ leaves all features at index $t$ completely unchanged.

### Q15: What features are fed into the XGBoost Regressor?
**Answer:** Exactly 7 ordered features:
1. `month_num`: Calendar month integer (1–12) for annual cyclicality.
2. `avg_price`: Average unit selling price in dollars.
3. `promo_days`: Active promotion days in target month.
4. `holiday_days`: Retail holiday days in target month.
5. `lag1`: Demand in month $t-1$.
6. `lag2`: Demand in month $t-2$.
7. `rolling3`: 3-month rolling average over months $t-1, t-2, t-3$.

### Q16: What three baseline models are evaluated, and what are their formulas?
**Answer:**
1. *Naive Forecast (Lag-1):* $\hat{y}_t = y_{t-1}$ (assumes demand remains constant from prior month).
2. *Seasonal-Naive Forecast (Lag-12):* $\hat{y}_t = y_{t-12}$ (assumes demand replicates the same calendar month of the prior year; falls back to lag-1 if missing).
3. *Moving Average (3-Month Rolling):* $\hat{y}_t = \frac{1}{3} \sum_{k=1}^3 y_{t-k}$ (smooths short-term fluctuations).

### Q17: Explain the formulas and practical differences between MAE, RMSE, MAPE, and WAPE.
**Answer:**
- **MAE (Mean Absolute Error):** $\frac{1}{N} \sum |y_i - \hat{y}_i|$. Measures average error magnitude in physical inventory units.
- **RMSE (Root Mean Squared Error):** $\sqrt{\frac{1}{N} \sum (y_i - \hat{y}_i)^2}$. Penalizes large outlier errors disproportionately due to squaring, vital for warehouse capacity limits.
- **Safe MAPE (Mean Absolute Percentage Error):** $\frac{100}{N_{y>0}} \sum_{y_i > 0} \frac{|y_i - \hat{y}_i|}{y_i}$. Masks zero actuals to prevent mathematical division by zero.
- **WAPE (Weighted Absolute Percentage Error):** $\frac{\sum |y_i - \hat{y}_i|}{\sum y_i} \times 100\%$. The supply-chain gold standard. Normalizes total absolute errors by total actual sales volume. Completely immune to zero-actual distortion and small-denominator inflation.

### Q18: What were the experimental findings across the four categories?
**Answer:**
- **Clothing:** XGBoost won (MAE: 37.18 vs Naive: 47.44; WAPE: 10.22% vs 13.04%).
- **Electronics:** XGBoost won (MAE: 24.40 vs Seasonal-Naive: 31.56; WAPE: 9.51% vs 12.29%).
- **Home:** XGBoost won (MAE: 45.43 vs Seasonal-Naive: 60.22; WAPE: 13.15% vs 17.43%).
- **Beauty:** **Baseline (Moving Average 3) WON** (MAE: 56.26 vs XGBoost: 82.24; WAPE: 15.17% vs 22.17%).

### Q19: Why did the Moving Average baseline outperform XGBoost in the Beauty category?
**Answer:** Demand in the *Beauty* category follows smooth, non-seasonal, non-promotional repeat replenishment patterns. Gradient-boosted decision trees overfit slight monthly fluctuations, whereas a simple 3-month moving average dampens noise more effectively. DemandIQ transparently reports this finding and selects the 3-Month Moving Average for Beauty replenishment, demonstrating scientific honesty over blind algorithmic bias.

### Q20: How are prediction intervals calculated, and how was the in-sample residual bug fixed?
**Answer:** Intervals are calculated as $\hat{y}_{t+1} \pm 1.96 \times s_{\text{test}}$.
In early development, $s$ was computed on in-sample training residuals ($\sigma_{\text{train}} \approx 2.0$), which produced empirical hold-out coverage of only 33%–54% because decision trees fit training data tightly. The audit corrected this by setting $s_{\text{test}}$ to the **out-of-sample hold-out test RMSE**, raising empirical test coverage to **94.4% across categories** (Clothing: 100%, Electronics: 100%, Beauty: 88.9%, Home: 88.9%).

## Section 4: Backend API & Decision Systems (Member 3 Lead)

### Q21: What is the role of Pydantic v2 in DemandIQ's FastAPI backend?
**Answer:** Pydantic v2 acts as the data validation and contract enforcement layer. It strictly parses incoming HTTP query parameters and JSON payloads, guarantees types, enforces finite-number constraints (`math.isfinite`) to reject `NaN` or `Infinity`, and validates that promotional/holiday days do not exceed physical calendar lengths for the given month.

### Q22: How does the backend prevent on-the-fly retraining during inference requests?
**Answer:** Through the singleton `ModelRegistry` class in `backend/app/ml.py`. During the FastAPI `lifespan` startup, `model_registry.preload_all()` deserializes all `.pkl` models into memory. When an endpoint such as `POST /api/predict/custom` or `GET /api/predict/category` is called, inference executes in $< 15\text{ms}$ using preloaded estimators with zero disk reads or model retraining.

### Q23: What formula is used to calculate Lead-Time Demand ($LTD$)?
**Answer:** Because the XGBoost model outputs a 30-day forward demand forecast ($D_{\text{forecast}}$), demand during supplier delivery lead time ($L$ days) is proportionally approximated as:
$$LTD = D_{\text{forecast}} \times \left(\frac{L}{30.0}\right)$$
The catalog records empirical supplier lead times ranging from 3 to 12 days (averaging ~5–8 days across categories). When vendor lead times are missing, a conservative **14-day default buffer** is applied.

### Q24: What are the formulas for Reorder Point ($ROP$) and Target Inventory?
**Answer:**
- **Reorder Point:** $ROP = LTD + SS$, where $SS$ is safety stock buffering against demand variance ($Z \times \sigma_L$).
- **Target Inventory:** $Target = D_{\text{forecast}} + SS$, representing the ideal inventory position required to cover 30 days of forward demand plus buffer.

### Q25: How was the reorder quantity cliff discontinuity defect resolved?
**Answer:** In early iterations, the reorder rule only triggered when $I_{\text{current}} < D_{\text{forecast}}$, causing a cliff where holding 1 unit above demand resulted in 0 reorder units, leaving safety stock depleted. Member 3 implemented the continuous target-buffer replenishment formula:
$$Q_{\text{reorder}} = \max\left(0, \lceil Target - I_{\text{current}} - I_{\text{incoming}} \rceil\right)$$
Whenever $I_{\text{current}} \le ROP$ or net available stock falls below safety stock. This replenishes stock smoothly without arbitrary boundary cliffs.

### Q26: Are purchase orders automatically submitted to suppliers?
**Answer:** **No. Automated purchasing is strictly disabled (`automated_ordering_enabled = false`).** DemandIQ operates exclusively as a decision-support advisory system. All suggested replenishment quantities require human managerial review and manual authorization through corporate procurement workflows.

### Q27: How is price elasticity calculated, and why is it non-causal?
**Answer:** Empirical price elasticity is calculated as:
$$\epsilon = \frac{\% \Delta \text{ Demand}}{\% \Delta \text{ Realized Price}}$$
It is strictly an **observational historical correlation**. The platform disclaims causality because promotional price drops were historically co-scheduled with marketing campaigns and holiday events. Furthermore, the database tracks retail unit prices and discounts but **does not record supplier wholesale costs of goods sold (COGS)**, making it mathematically impossible to calculate net margins or claim causal profit maximization.

### Q28: How does the backend handle HTTP error codes?
**Answer:**
- `400 Bad Request`: Invalid business logic parameters.
- `404 Not Found`: Request for an unsupported category (e.g., `/api/predict/category?category=Automotive`).
- `422 Unprocessable Entity`: Pydantic validation failures (e.g., negative prices, days $> 31$, infinite values).
- `503 Service Unavailable`: Missing model artifacts on disk prior to training.

## Section 5: Frontend UI/UX & Conversational AI (Member 4 Lead)

### Q29: How does the React 19 frontend optimize performance and bundle size?
**Answer:** Member 4 implemented route-level code splitting using `React.lazy()` and `Suspense`. Instead of bundling all 9 dashboard pages into a monolithic JavaScript file, Vite splits pages into distinct chunks loaded on demand when the user navigates to a route. A consistent `<LoadingState>` component renders during chunk transitions.

### Q30: How does the unified Axios API client handle server errors?
**Answer:** In `frontend/src/api.js`, an Axios instance configures a 15-second timeout and registers response interceptors. When the backend returns an error (such as a 422 or 503), the interceptor extracts the normalized error message (`err.response?.data?.detail`) and bubbles it to UI `<ErrorState>` components with actionable retry buttons.

### Q31: What is the Structured AI Insights Schema Contract?
**Answer:** In `backend/app/ai.py`, `generate_structured_insights()` enforces a 10-field contract:
1. `id`: Deterministic identifier (e.g., `INS-INV-CLOTHING-STOCKOUT`).
2. `title`: Concise business headline.
3. `category`: Domain tag (`demand_trends`, `inventory_risk`, `pricing_promotion`, `forecast_limitations`).
4. `severity`: `CRITICAL`, `WARNING`, `MEDIUM`, or `INFO`.
5. `supporting_metrics`: Dictionary of genuine numeric values with explicit units.
6. `recommendation`: Concrete, non-automated action.
7. `data_source`: Underlying calculation method.
8. `evidence_quality`: Classification label (`HIGH_EMPIRICAL`, `MODEL_BENCHMARK`, `ESTIMATED_RUN_RATE`).
9. `limitations`: Explicit boundary conditions.
10. `period`: Time horizon reference.

### Q32: How does the system prevent contradictions in AI insights?
**Answer:** Deterministic logic barriers are enforced:
- A category flagged with `stockout_risk` can never simultaneously emit an `overstock_surplus` insight.
- Categories with low seasonal variance ($< 5\%$) are suppressed from high-seasonality alerts.
- All insight objects are deduplicated by their deterministic ID before serialization.

### Q33: How does the Business Assistant prevent generative hallucinations?
**Answer:** The conversational assistant (`answer_business_question`) is strictly data-grounded. It extracts verified metrics directly from backend services (`get_inventory_intelligence`, `get_category_trends`, `get_model_benchmark`). If an external LLM is enabled (Gemini or Ollama), it is supplied with a strict prompt containing only pre-aggregated metrics and forbidden from introducing unverified numbers.

### Q34: How does the Assistant handle queries regarding absent data (e.g., wholesale COGS)?
**Answer:** It applies intent triggers for keywords like `"wholesale"`, `"cogs"`, `"supplier cost"`, or `"margin"`. Rather than hallucinating a number, it returns an explicit `UNAVAILABLE` status, explaining that the database tracks sales prices and discounts but does not contain wholesale costs or profit margins, advising the user to consult finance.

### Q35: How does the Assistant handle short or ambiguous queries like "price?" or "help"?
**Answer:** Queries with two words or fewer are classified as `AMBIGUOUS`. The assistant provides a helpful response outlining the four operational areas it can analyze (inventory risk, demand trends, pricing observations, forecast limitations) and asks a clarifying question to guide the user.

### Q36: What happens if an external language model (Gemini or Ollama) is completely offline?
**Answer:** DemandIQ incorporates a zero-downtime **deterministic analytical fallback** (`_answer_question_fallback`). When external LLM APIs are unreachable or timeout, the engine synthesizes grounded responses directly from database records, ensuring 100% platform availability without external dependencies.

## Section 6: Testing, Trade-Offs & Limitations

### Q37: What is the scope and pass rate of the automated test suite?
**Answer:** The test suite contains **81 automated test cases with a 100% pass rate (81 passed in ~5 minutes)** using Pytest. It spans:
- Dataset invariants (8 tests)
- Analytics transformations (8 tests)
- ML leakage, training, and metrics (18 tests)
- Inventory policies and pricing rules (9 tests)
- AI insights and assistant guardrails (22 tests)
- API endpoint contracts and HTTP validation (16 tests)

### Q38: Why was XGBoost chosen over Deep Learning (LSTM/Transformers) or Prophet?
**Answer:**
1. *Short Sequence Suitability:* Deep learning architectures (LSTMs, Temporal Fusion Transformers) require tens of thousands of time steps to avoid severe overfitting. DemandIQ's 48-month dataset (36 training months) is ideally suited for tree-based ensemble methods.
2. *Exogenous Feature Interactions:* Facebook Prophet struggles with non-linear feature interactions between price, promotions, and holidays. XGBoost captures non-linear tabular interactions efficiently.
3. *Latency & Memory:* XGBoost models serialize to $< 200\text{KB}$ pickle files and execute inference in milliseconds on standard CPUs without GPU dependencies.

### Q39: What are the primary known limitations of the current implementation?
**Answer:**
1. *Synthetic Data:* Dataset ends in December 2024 and does not reflect macroeconomic shocks or supply chain disruptions.
2. *Single-Month Forecast Horizon:* Models predict one month forward ($t+1$). Multi-step recursive forecasting compounds uncertainty.
3. *Untracked Supplier Invoices:* Absence of wholesale COGS prevents true profit-margin optimization.
4. *Incoming Shipments:* Purchase orders in transit are untracked (assumed $0$).

### Q40: What are the key future enhancements planned for DemandIQ?
**Answer:**
1. Migration from SQLite to PostgreSQL for distributed enterprise workloads.
2. Recursive multi-horizon forecasting (3-month and 6-month demand trajectories).
3. Integration with ERP/WMS APIs (SAP, Oracle NetSuite) for live on-hand inventory and in-transit purchase order tracking.
4. Causal Bayesian econometric modeling for true price elasticity and markdown optimization.
